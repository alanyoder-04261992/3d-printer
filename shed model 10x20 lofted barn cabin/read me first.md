The times and grams are Flash Studio's own for each plate (sliced three times on Nov 4 2026 with
the 79 1/2 in walls, with every bridge modifier below in; the same in every slice): about 8 1/3
hours for `2 all plates.3mf`, with about 104 g of white, 76 g of black and 65 g of brown. Flash
Studio gave no warnings on either file.

Start Flash Studio first, then open them with **File → Open Project** (Ctrl+O), or **Open
Project** on its Home page. Starting Flash Studio by opening the file itself can leave it stuck
on "Loading". If Flash Studio says the file is from a newer version, click OK; it loads fine. If
it asks about unsaved changes to your settings, click **Discard** (the file brings its own). The
files carry their settings: the Flashforge AD5X 0.4, `0.20mm Standard @FF AD5X` with **Wall
generator → Arachne** (the siding is only 1.4 mm thick, and Arachne fills thin walls solid) and
**Elephant foot compensation** 0.1 mm, and three filaments, 1 white, 2 black and 3 brown. Keep
the elephant foot at 0.1: the posts, barbs and stops under the walls print on the bed and fit
with 0.2 mm to spare. Pick a plate, then Slice plate and Print plate as usual.

**Check the slots in the Send dialog every time.** Flash Studio matches each filament to the slot
whose colour on the printer is closest, and the printer still shows the old colours. Click each
filament's slot box and pick the slot by number: 1 white, 2 black, 3 brown.

**Brims are set in the file.** The two skids are long and thin, so each has a 5 mm brim, and they
are laid out far enough apart that the brims don't touch. The corner angles, roof sheets, porch
frame, posts and railings are set to no brim, so no lip shows on an edge you see. Nothing needs
supports.

**Bridge modifiers, leave them in:** the four walls and the porch gable, and the fit test's short
walls, doorway and gable foot, each carry a modifier, "bridges across the studs". It sets
**External bridge infill direction** to 180, which lays the siding's first layer straight across
the studs (16.3 mm at most in the slices). Left to itself Flash Studio sometimes laid it along the
stud bays in strands about 60 mm long, which sag. On the back wall and the porch gable it stops at the collar tie, and a
second one, "bridges up the gable", lays the two triangles over it from the collar tie up (11 mm at
most; across they'd be 31 mm). The floor carries seven, "bridges across the seams", on its second
layer only. It prints face down, and left to itself Flash Studio laid the bridge over each floor
sheet seam that runs across the plate along the seam, in strands up to 39 mm; now every seam and
deck-board groove is bridged straight across, except the last 2 mm of a seam where it runs into
another, and no strand over a seam hangs free for more than 2.1 mm. Don't turn the walls or the
floor on the plate: the modifiers lay the bridges along the bed, not along the part, so a wall
turned a quarter turn would get the long strands every time.

Each wall prints lying flat, inside face down, so its trim (door and window casings, the band
across the back wall, the rake boards under the roof edges, the faux loft window) is on top and
already set to **filament 2**; the siding is filament 1. All the trim is in the top 0.6 mm and
nothing white stands any higher, so plates 1 and 3 change colour once, for the last three layers.
The door is all white. On plates 1 and 3 the prime tower goes in the back right
corner, and those plates are laid out to keep it clear; plates 2 and 4 are one colour and need no
tower. The porch frame prints with the roof, so if you want a grey roof, the frame and the corner
angles come out grey too.

The `.stl` files are the same pieces one at a time, all one colour, if you'd rather set up a
plate yourself (`right side.stl` and `left side.stl` are the long walls; `upper roof.stl`,
`lower roof.stl`, `loft.stl` and `side railing.stl` each print twice, `porch post.stl` three
times and `front railing.stl` once; `floor.stl` and `loft.stl` are already upside down;

bed). A plate set up from `.stl` files gets none of the settings the `.3mf` files carry: set
**Wall generator → Arachne** yourself, and set the posts, railings, corner angles, roof sheets
and porch frame to no brim. For two colours, and for the walls' and floor's bridge settings, use
the `.3mf` files.

## How it holds together

- **The walls stand on the floor.** The side walls and the back wall have their siding carried
  on down past the floor as a skirt, hiding the floor's edge. The front wall stands 4 ft back
  from the front edge, under the porch, right on the deck: it has no skirt. Its posts and its door
  sill go down into closed pockets in the floor, and its two end columns into notches at the
  floor's front corners.
- **Posts and prongs.** Under every stud a short post drops into a notch or pocket in the floor.
  Ten prongs go down slots in the floor: three under each side wall, two under the front wall and
  two under the back wall. Each prong is a springy leg with a barb on its foot: going down, the
  barb pushes the leg out; at the bottom it springs back under a ledge with a click. Beside each
  leg a stiff stop keeps the wall's foot from sliding out. The snap is the barn's exactly.
- **Front wall first, back wall last.** The front wall goes on first, with its door already hung
  in it. Each side wall then comes straight down behind the front wall's end columns. The lofts go
  in next, and the back wall last, because its gable stands tall right over its corners.
- **The front corners are glued butt joints.** There is no corner board behind the porch (the
  3D designer draws none there).
  The front wall runs right through to the corner. Each side wall's flat front end stands 0.1 mm
  behind the front wall's end column. The joint sits in the bottom of a siding groove: the side
  wall's edge is notched and the end column's edge is cut off at 45 degrees, so you see a groove
  like the others, not a gap. Thin super glue wicked into the hairline holds it.
- **The back corners have black corner angles,** as on the barn (Alan, Nov 3 2026). Each wall
  has a recess 0.4 mm deep (two layers) beside the corner, and an L angle glues into both. Each
  leg covers 3.0 mm of its wall. Once glued, the four walls are one box.
- **The side walls lean.** A side wall has most of its weight in its siding, out past the
  floor's edge, so each side wall leans out about 2 mm at the top on its own. The lofts sitting on
  both side walls' top plates hold them upright.
- **The lofts** rest on the side walls' top plates, one just behind the front wall and one at the
  back, each 4 ft deep. Two lugs under each loft at each wall drop into notches that go right
  through the top plates. A loft goes in either way round.
- **The porch.** The three brown posts stand dry in sockets in the deck. The black porch frame (a
  beam across the front, a beam down each side and one along the front wall) is glued onto the
  posts' top tenons and, at the back ends of its side beams, to the front wall's face. So the
  walls and the whole porch lift off the floor together. The white ceiling lies loose on a lip
  inside the frame. The porch gable stands on the frame's front beam on two pegs. The railings are
  glued into holes in the deck; their ends stand 0.2 mm off the posts. The one front railing runs
  across the left bay, under the window; the right bay, in front of the door, is open.
- **The roof** is four sheets, as on the barn: a lower sheet on each steep edge of the two gables
  (the back wall and the porch gable), on pegs standing straight up, and an upper sheet on each
  shallow slope, its tail lapping over the lower sheet. Every sheet only comes off straight up,
  and the upper sheets lock the lower ones on.
- **The door swings in.** It hangs on two pins in the front wall, on the right of its doorway as
  seen from the porch, next to the right side wall, so its knob is on the left, toward the middle
  of the wall. That is your answer for the Cabin (Nov 4 2026, "hinges on the right side"); the two
  models share the door, this one 2.1 in shorter. You hang it before that wall goes on the floor: the bottom pin goes in
  along a little slot in the threshold from the inside, and once the wall is on the floor, the
  floor closes that slot. So the door stays in until the walls come off. It swings in toward the
  right side wall to about 102 degrees, where it touches the doorway's right-hand stud, clear of
  that wall (6.5 mm, about 8 in) and under the front loft. Shut, it rests against a strip on the
  latch side (the left), so it can't swing out.
- **The skids have a front end.** Their two front corners are cut off; that end goes under the
  porch. Laid the other way, the pegs miss their holes.

## Checking the fit

Before you start the big prints, print both plates of `1 fit test.3mf` and try them:

1. **Porch joints (plate 1).** Set the deck piece face up with the board grooves running away
   from you and the post's socket in the near right-hand corner: the edge toward you is the
   porch's front edge, the one on your right its side edge. (It is the right front corner, where
   the way in is, so it has the side railing's hole but no front railing hole; the front railing's
   holes are the same size.) The post's tenons are small tabs flush with one face of it: turn the
   post so that face is toward the right-hand side edge, not toward you, and push its bottom tenon
   into the socket. It should go in by hand and the post should stand square. Drop the frame
   corner onto the post's top tenon: it should sit flat on the post by itself, its side edge
   flush with the deck's and its front edge standing a little (0.6 mm) proud of the deck's front
   edge, like the real header band. Put the gable foot's peg into the other socket, the one in the
   frame corner's top: the foot should sit flat, and the two should still stand on the post by
   themselves (they are cut small so they balance there). Lift them off again: on the short post
   the frame corner is where the railing end goes. Push the railing end's peg into its hole by the
   post, cap toward the middle of the deck: it should push in by hand until it bottoms, not tight
   and not dropping in loose. It stands on that one peg, so it leans over; that's expected (the
   real railings have two pegs, and you hold them upright while the glue sets). Hold it upright,
   and its end should stand about 0.2 mm off the post. If the post stands off its spot (past the
   deck's edge, or in the railing end's way), it is turned wrong.
2. **Front corner (plate 2).** Press the short front wall (the piece with the edge of the door's
   casing) down onto the bit of floor: its end column goes into the corner notch and the end of
   the door's sill into the end of its closed pocket, without forcing, and its prong should
   click. Then lower the short right side
   straight down behind it, its flat end sliding down the back of the front wall's end column,
   until its prong clicks. The joint should sit in the bottom of a groove. Look at the top of the
   short right side's front end, on the inside: there is a little notch there that the front
   loft's lug drops into, with plate printed over it. If that plate sagged down into the notch, the
   real side walls will do the same: shave it flat as in "Cleaning up the parts".
3. **Door (plate 2).** The little door hangs on the right, like the real one. Hang it in the
   little doorway from the inside, its pins on your left, the same way as the real one (step 1 of
   "Putting it together"). The little doorway's inside is the face that
   was on the plate, the one the little slot in the threshold runs out through. It should swing in
   freely, and shut it should stop against the strip on the latch side. Look at that face: there
   should be no slot over the top hole.
4. **Roof peg (plate 2).** Stand the peg block up so the peg points up and set the bit of roof on
   it. It should drop on and lift off without forcing. The bit of roof has a round hole like the
   upper sheets'. The lower sheets' holes run through the steep sheet on a slant, so each is a slot
   about 8 mm long with a bridged roof, and the fit test doesn't try those (see "Cleaning up the
   parts").

Pin holes are 0.35 mm bigger all round than their pins (2.7 mm for a 2.0 mm pin), peg holes
under the roof 0.4 mm all round, the porch's tenons and pegs have 0.35 mm round, the snap parts
0.2 mm, and the corner angles about 0.1 mm in their recesses. If anything is too tight or too
loose, tell Claude which and ask to change that gap by 0.1 mm (0.05 for the corner angles or the
barbs) and remake the files. The sizes are at the top of `make_barn_cabin.py`.

Two fits aren't in the fit test. The back corner angles fit their recesses the way the barn
model's do (the same thickness, recess depth and clearance, only narrower); you try them dry at
step 10, and if one won't go in, only the two small angles need printing again, a little smaller. The skids' pegs are 2.0 mm in 2.4 mm holes, 0.2 mm round, a
snug push fit as on the shed, the barn and the Cabin; if one is tight at step 16, twist a 2.5 mm
drill bit through that floor hole by hand.

## Cleaning up the parts

- Let the plate cool right down before you flex the parts off. Don't push a scraper under the
  ends of a wall or under the prongs: each prong's leg is a thin strip lifted just off the plate.
  Flex the plate to free the corner angles, posts and railings; don't bend them.
- With a fresh hobby knife, take a light chamfer off the first-layer lip (the edge that was on
  the plate) on: the posts, stops and barbs under each wall, the front wall's end columns, the
  inside edge of the back mitres, the floor's face all round its edge, the roof pegs on both
  gables, the porch gable's two pegs, the door's two pins, the posts' tenons and the railings'
  pegs, the mouth of every socket and peg hole in the deck and of the porch frame's three post
  sockets (the deck and the frame print face down), and the mouths of the peg holes under each
  roof sheet. A lip on any of those makes it tight.
  Don't scrape the mitre faces or the side walls' flat front ends, and don't trim the barbs' flat
  tops.
- Look down each back corner recess and pick out any string or blob, so the angles sit flat. Pick
  any string or blob out of the door's little slot in the threshold too.
- Look into the two long slanted holes under each lower roof sheet. If the roof of one sagged,
  shave it with the knife, so the sheet sits right down on its peg.
- At the top front corner of each side wall, on the inside, the first loft notch runs out through
  the wall's front end, and the plate over it printed with nothing under it on two sides. If it
  sagged into the notch, shave its underside flat with the knife, so the front loft's lug drops in.
- The corner angles' outside face on the side wall was on the plate. If you can see a lip along
  its edges, shave it off with the knife held flat.
- The posts print lying down with no brim. If one lifted at its ends, print it again with a brim
  and shave the lip off.
- The balusters are only 1.1 mm wide. Clean strings off the railings with flush cutters; don't sand.
- Snip any loose strands between the studs on the inside. Cut strings in the windows and the door's
  lites where they join, holding the thin bars from behind. Don't sand the outside; it fills the grooves.
- Lay each wall on glass. If it rocks, set the bed to 55 °C, lay the wall framing-side down on it
  under a sheet of paper and a flat board with about 1 kg on top, leave it 10 minutes, then let it
  cool before lifting the weight.

## Putting it together

You'll need your fingers, two rubber bands that go round the walls, thin (water-thin) super glue
for the front corners, gel super glue or 5-minute epoxy for the corner angles and the porch, and
tweezers or a small flat screwdriver if you ever take it apart. `how it goes together.png` shows
the order.

1. **Door into the front wall, before that wall goes on the floor.** It stays in from now on.
   Hold the front wall upright, its inside toward you. Hold the door with its knob away from you
   (toward the porch side), its pins on your left (the right as seen from the porch, the end of
   the wall that meets the right side wall), the longer pin at the top. Push the top pin up into
   the hole in the top of the doorway and keep pushing up until the door's top meets the top of
   the doorway, about half a millimetre. Swing the bottom toward the wall, along the little slot
   in the threshold, until the bottom pin is over its hole, and let it drop. It swings in, toward
   you and round to your left, and shut it rests against the strip on the latch side (on your
   right). If a pin is stiff, take the door out again and twist
   a 2.7 mm drill bit by hand in that pin's hole now, before the wall goes on the floor; never
   lever the door.
2. **Floor.** Lay the floor face up on a flat table, without the skids.
3. **Front wall**, with its door. Hold the door shut and lower the wall onto the line of pockets
   4 ft back from the front edge, door side out, its two end columns into the notches at the
   corners. Press down over its two prongs until each clicks.
4. **Left side.** Lower it straight down at the left edge (seen from the porch), its flat front
   end sliding down the back of the front wall's end column, until its three prongs click. It
   leans out a little at the top; that's fine for now.
5. **Right side.** The same way on the other side. It leans out a little too.
6. **Lofts.** Push both side walls straight. Drop one loft in just behind the front wall and one at
   the back end, floor side up, lugs into the notches. Either way round fits.
7. **Back wall.** Lower it straight down at the back, past the back loft and the side walls'
   mitred ends, until its two prongs click.
8. **Rubber bands.** Put a rubber band round all four walls about 15 mm above the table and
   another about 10 mm under the side walls' top, just tight enough to close the back mitres. Look
   along each side: the side wall flush with the front wall's end column, the groove at the front
   corner even top to bottom.
9. **Front corners, rubber bands on.** Lift the front loft straight out first: its lugs sit in
   notches right at the top of the front joints, and glue there would fix it in for good. The bands
   and the back loft hold the side walls straight meanwhile. Touch a small drop of thin super glue
   to each front joint, the hairline between the side wall's end and the back of the front wall, at
   its inside edge about 5 mm under the top (below the loft's notch), and let it wick down. If it
   doesn't get halfway, put a second small drop there. Don't put any glue lower than halfway: at
   the floor the joint runs down into the floor's corner notch, and glue there would fix the walls
   to the floor (if you see it get down there, wipe it off at once). Leave the bands on 10 minutes,
   then drop the front loft back in.
10. **Back corner angles.** The angles come in two hands, one for each back corner. Hold one with
    its foot down: the leg whose top steps up goes on the back wall, just under the rake board,
    and the other leg on the side wall. Try both dry first: ease the bands out from the corner,
    press the angle straight into its two recesses with its foot on the table, and let the bands
    back over it. Then glue them one at a time: 3 or 4 pin-head dots of gel super glue down each
    recess floor, in the half nearest the corner. Press the angle back in, foot on the table, bands
    over it. Leave the bands on 15 minutes (an hour for epoxy). Then take both rubber bands off
    (slide them up and off, or snip them): once the porch frame is on, they are hard to get past it.
11. **Railings, before the posts.** The two short ones (7 balusters, both from
    `side railing.stl`) go down the sides of the porch. The long one (9 balusters) goes across the
    left bay, between the left corner post's socket and the middle post's socket, under the
    window. All three go cap toward the middle of the porch, their flat face (the one their pegs
    are flush with) toward the deck's edge. The right bay, in front of the door, stays open: it is
    the way in. Try each one dry first. Then put a drop of glue in each peg hole and press the pegs
    home: they bottom in their holes. On its pegs alone a railing can lean, so hold each one
    upright, square to the deck, for half a minute while the glue grabs (longer for epoxy).
12. **Posts.** Stand the three in their sockets in the deck, dry. Each post's tenons are small
    tabs flush with one face of it: turn the right corner post so that face is toward the right
    side wall, and the left corner post and the middle post so it is toward the left side wall,
    never toward the front, the back or the way in. A post turned any other way still drops into
    its socket, but it stands off its spot: up against a railing or out past the deck's edge.
13. **Porch frame.** Put a small drop of gel super glue (or 5-minute epoxy) on each post's top
    tenon. Lower the frame straight down over them, guiding each tenon into its socket, until it
    sits on all three. The back ends of its side beams then stand a hair (0.2 mm) off the front
    wall: leave that gap, don't push them against it (that would lean the posts). Put one drop of
    glue into each of those two gaps. Don't move it until it has set: the deck holds each post's
    foot and the frame its top.
14. **Ceiling.** Drop it into the frame onto the lip, the face that was on the plate down (it is
    the smooth one, seen from under the porch).
15. **Porch gable.** Vent out, its two pegs into the frame's front beam, a dot of glue on each.
16. **Skids.** Lay them notches and pegs up, front ends (corners cut off, peg nearer the end) the

## Taking it apart

Lift the upper roof sheets straight off, then the lower ones. Lift out the ceiling and the lofts.
The four walls, the porch frame, the posts, the porch gable and the door are now one unit. Lift
the cabin off its skids and look underneath: just inside each wall you can see the barbs in their
slots, ten in all (three under each side wall, two under the front wall, two under the back wall).
Push each barb out toward the siding with a small flat screwdriver, only just far enough to clear
its ledge (about half a millimetre), and ease that side up a little. When all ten are free, lift
the unit straight up off the floor; the posts' feet slide out of their sockets past the railings.
With it off the floor, the door comes off the way it went on.
