# Choosing a material

The numbers (temps, density) live in `materials.toml`, which the checker reads.
This page is about which one to pick.

| Use | Pick | Why |
|-----|------|-----|
| Indoor prototype, fit test, scale model | **PLA** | Cheapest, easiest, crisp detail |
| Hardware on a shed (latches, vents, brackets) | **PETG** | Tough, handles weather |
| Parts in full sun all day (signs, trim, lot displays) | **ASA** | Best UV resistance. Needs an enclosed printer |
| Bumpers, door stops, feet, gaskets | **TPU** | Flexible |

Rules:

- **Never PLA outside or in a closed shed.** It softens around 55 °C. A shed in summer gets hotter than that.
- Prototype in PLA to check the fit, then print the real part in PETG or ASA. PETG shrinks a little more than PLA, so re-check tight fits.
- Keep PETG, TPU and ASA dry. Wet filament strings, pops and prints weak.
